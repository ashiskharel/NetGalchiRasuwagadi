The casualty table is off this board. What is logged now is rain the forecasts called, and water the Department has measured.

In the 24 hours up to about 13:00 Nepal time on 31 October, the Department’s rainfall watch has Taal in Manang at 125.6 mm, very heavy on the Department’s own scale. Godavari in Lalitpur is at 98 mm. Bhorleni in Makawanpur is at 60.6 mm. Thori in Parsa is at 75 mm. Chandrapur in Rautahat, the nearest reporting station to the Bagmati Terai model point, is at 28.8 mm, which is moderate. OnlineKhabar, quoting the Department, had Tal at 104.4 mm at 06:24 the same morning. These are trailing 24-hour totals, not a model’s calendar day, and Saturday afternoon is not over.

On the rivers at the same hour, Bagmati at Khokana reads 3.588 m, above its 3.50 m warning line and under the 4.00 m danger line, steady. Lal Bakiya at Jureli Bazar is just over its warning line and rising. Bagmati at Samanpur, on the Terai, is 85.208 m against a warning of 89 m. Ratu at Lalgadh is 217.037 m against a warning of 218 m. Karnali at Chisapani is 4.65 m against a warning of 10 m. Koshi at Chatara and Narayani at Narayanghat Bridge sent no level in this pull.

ECMWF’s Friday and Saturday calls at the Bagmati Terai cell were 70.5 mm and 118 mm. ICON’s Saturday call was 52.9 mm. GFS’s Saturday call was 5.3 mm. Those rows stay pending. The gauges are not on that cell, and their clock is not those two calendar days. The Department’s broader wording, moderate in many places and heavy in some, is holding on the stations above. It is not a final score.
